Helping Customers Stay Connected Abroad

A mobile-first roaming assistant designed to help customers understand charges, manage spend caps and access support before and during overseas travel.

Role
Product Designer
Platforms
Mobile web, mobile app
Responsibilities
UX, service design, information architecture
Deliverables
Research, user flows, wireframes, visual concepts

Overview

Clearer roaming, before and during the trip.

International roaming can be confusing, particularly for people travelling abroad for the first time or visiting destinations with unfamiliar charging structures. This concept explored how a digital assistant could make essential roaming guidance easier to find.

By bringing destination information, cost guidance and support together, the experience helps customers prepare before travelling and find useful next steps while overseas.

The challenge

Make complex roaming easier to understand.

Customers experienced bill shock before, during and after overseas travel. Unclear destination information and poorly understood spend caps contributed to dissatisfaction and a high volume of support calls.

The opportunity was to provide relevant guidance earlier, make common tasks easier to find and help customers try self-service before contacting support.

My role

End-to-end concept design

  • Analysed requirements and existing customer support themes
  • Reviewed competitor experiences and identified usability opportunities
  • Designed customer journeys, information architecture and user flows
  • Created wireframes, prototypes and high-fidelity visual concepts

Understanding the problem

Recurring support needs pointed to a clearer journey.

01

Support data

Spend caps, roaming tariffs and technical issues while travelling were recurring reasons customers contacted support.

02

Customer behaviour

People struggled to find destination-specific details, felt overwhelmed by lengthy help pages and often skipped self-service before contacting support.

03

Competitive analysis

Existing utility apps could be overly technical or promotional, request permissions too early, and do little to explain results or next steps.

Approach

Give travellers the right help at the right time.

01

Search by destination

Travellers often know where they are going before they know what to ask. Starting with a destination search and popular locations helps them reach relevant guidance quickly.

02

Reveal information progressively

Show information relevant to the customer's destination, package and plans rather than presenting every roaming detail at once.

03

Prevent problems early

Encourage customers to review charges, understand roaming products and check spend caps before travel, shifting the experience from reactive support to prevention.

Information architecture

Organised around the tasks travellers need to complete.

A simple task-based structure brings together destination information, roaming costs and support.

Design system

A clear, mobile-first visual language.

Readability, strong hierarchy and familiar patterns keep common roaming tasks approachable on a small screen.

Visual language

A simple, readable interface with strong hierarchy and large touch targets helps people complete common actions quickly.

Task-based structure

The experience is organised around three key tasks: find destination information, understand costs and get help.

Useful interface patterns

Destination search, spend-cap calculators, troubleshooting cards, expandable help and contextual recommendations support common needs.

Consistent layouts

Layouts, typography and interactions follow an 8-point grid system to maintain consistency throughout the experience.

Key features

Support for the moments that matter.

Destination search

ChallengeCustomers struggled to locate information relevant to where they were travelling.

Design responseA destination-first search with popular places and predictive suggestions.

Intended benefitMake relevant roaming information easier to find.

Dynamic spend-cap guidance

ChallengeSpend caps were a common source of confusion and support calls.

Design responseHelp customers estimate how their current cap relates to the length of their trip.

Intended benefitGive customers a clearer view of roaming costs before travelling.

Roaming assistant

ChallengeGuidance was spread across multiple pages and support articles.

Design responseBring costs, spend caps, package details, troubleshooting and travel guidance into one place.

Intended benefitMake roaming information easier to navigate without relying on support.

Support when issues arise

ChallengeCustomers often contacted support before trying digital self-service.

Design responseSurface troubleshooting journeys and relevant help topics within the roaming experience.

Intended benefitGuide customers towards possible resolutions before escalating.

Selected screens

A connected journey from search to support.

Early wireframes explored landing pages, destination selection, country details and spend-cap interactions. User flows mapped research before travel, roaming setup, cost understanding, troubleshooting and spend-cap updates before the high-fidelity concepts were developed.

Impact

A concept focused on preventing roaming problems.

This concept project explored how better information architecture, proactive guidance and self-service design could address a complex support problem. It did not establish measured customer or business results.

Opportunity

Reduce customer effort

Make destination-specific information easier to locate and understand.

Opportunity

Build confidence

Help travellers understand charges and prepare for their trip before they leave.

Opportunity

Encourage self-service

Present troubleshooting steps and relevant support topics before customers need to contact a support centre.

Opportunity

Prevent bill shock

Make costs and spend-cap information clearer before issues arise during a trip.

What I learned

Start with evidence, then simplify the journey.

Starting with support data, designing around specific customer tasks and reducing content overload helped shape a more approachable concept. The work also highlighted the challenge of balancing business needs with clear explanations of complex roaming costs.

If I revisited the project, I would test the journeys with frequent travellers, explore destination-history personalisation, add more contextual support recommendations and investigate live network and roaming status information.
